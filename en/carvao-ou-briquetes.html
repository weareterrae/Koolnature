Lump charcoal or briquettes? The guide to choosing well

Not rivals — different tools: charcoal gives fast, fierce heat, briquettes give hours of consistency. Choose by the cook, not by habit.

Biocharcoal: the sprint

Lights in 15–20 minutes, reaches high temperatures and responds quickly to airflow. It's the choice for searing, fast grilling and grill marks: steaks, sardines, shellfish, vegetables, skewers. If your barbecue lasts up to an hour and wants serious heat, this is it.

Briquettes: the marathon

Uniform in shape and density, briquettes burn slower and steadier — fewer spikes, more predictability. They're the choice for low & slow and long sessions: ribs, spatchcocked chicken, clay pots, cataplanas, smoking. Where lump asks for refills, the briquette keeps going.

And together?

The masters' trick: a briquette base for consistency + lump on top for the launch. You get instant searing heat and a foundation that lasts all afternoon. In a two-zone fire, briquettes on the indirect side and lump on the direct side is an unbeatable pair.

Frequently asked questions

Which burns hotter: lump charcoal or briquettes?

Lump reaches higher peaks, faster — ideal for searing. Briquettes peak lower but hold their temperature far longer.

Can I grill steaks over briquettes?

You can, but lump does it better: steak wants thermal shock to build a crust. Briquettes shine in long cooks and indirect heat.

Can I mix charcoal and briquettes?

You can and should, in long sessions: briquette base to last, lump on top to start fast and sear.
